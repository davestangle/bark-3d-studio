Before You Shoot

Getting the photo right
is everything.

01
Get outside.

Natural light is the single biggest factor. Overcast is ideal. Indoor lighting, flash, or window shadows will hurt the output. If you can only use one tip, it's this one.

Natural light only
02
Get on their level.

Crouch down. Camera at eye level with your dog. Shooting from above distorts body proportions. The AI needs to see the full silhouette to build accurate geometry.

Eye level, full body
03
Get them to sit still.

Motion blur is the enemy. Get them sitting or standing still, facing you or at a slight angle. A treat behind the lens works. Plain background behind them.

Still & sharp
What works
  • Outside on an overcast day, full body visible
  • Dog sitting or standing still, looking toward camera
  • Plain background — grass, concrete, a fence
  • Camera at their eye level, not above
  • Sharp focus, no blur, good exposure
What doesn't
  • Indoor photos with mixed or overhead lighting
  • Shooting from above — kills the proportions
  • Busy backgrounds with furniture, other dogs, people
  • Motion blur from a moving dog
  • Cropped ears, cropped tail, cut-off legs
