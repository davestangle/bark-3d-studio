Before You Shoot

The better your photo,
the better your sculpture.

01
Go outside.

Natural light makes everything better. Overcast days are perfect. Five minutes in the backyard beats any indoor photo. This is the single biggest thing you can do.

Natural light only
02
Get on their level.

Crouch down until your camera is at their eye level. Shooting from above makes them look squished. You want to see their full body -- head, chest, legs, tail.

Eye level, full body
03
Get them to hold still.

Hold a treat just above your phone. Get them in a sit. The cleaner the background behind them, the better -- grass, pavement, a blank wall all work great.

Still & sharp
What works
  • Outside on an overcast day, full body visible
  • Dog sitting or standing still, looking toward camera
  • Plain background — grass, concrete, a fence
  • Camera at their eye level, not above
  • Sharp focus, no blur, good exposure
What doesn't
  • Indoor photos with mixed or overhead lighting
  • Shooting from above — kills the proportions
  • Busy backgrounds with furniture, other dogs, people
  • Motion blur from a moving dog
  • Cropped ears, cropped tail, cut-off legs
